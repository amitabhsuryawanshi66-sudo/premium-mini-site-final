# Component Variation Decisions

Decisions made for the Premium Obsidian Ink Studio Transformation:

### Hero / Brand Entrance
- **Direction 1 (Rejected)**: Minimalist fashion-style hero with large centered text and wide whitespace. (Too "clean" for the gritty, textured studio vibe).
- **Direction 2 (Rejected)**: Full-screen video background with overlay text. (Risk of generic "luxury" feeling; less "editorial").
- **Direction 3 (Selected)**: Editorial Brand Mark + Asymmetric Media Collage.
- **Why**: The brand mark (bespoke CSS emblem) creates an immediate premium identity. The collage of process-led imagery (hands, ink) communicates craft better than a single abstract shot.

### Decision Dock (Control Panel)
- **Direction 1 (Rejected)**: Standard floating chips. (Feels like a mobile-app template).
- **Direction 2 (Rejected)**: Vertical list of large buttons. (Too much vertical scroll).
- **Direction 3 (Selected)**: Mechanical Concierge Control Panel.
- **Why**: By styling it like a physical tool or studio control, it reinforces the "bespoke appointment-only" nature of the business. It feels precise and intentional.

### Consultation Pathways
- **Direction 1 (Rejected)**: 4 equal cards in a 2x2 grid. (Fails to prioritize the most important services).
- **Direction 2 (Rejected)**: Accordion list. (Too text-heavy; loses the image-rich goal).
- **Direction 3 (Selected)**: Featured Media Strip followed by Asymmetric Mini-Cards.
- **Why**: Creates a "Journey" feel. The featured strip for "Fine-Line Concept" gives high-impact visual weight to the studio's primary niche.

### Concept Wall
- **Direction 1 (Rejected)**: Standard Pinterest-style masonry grid. (Too "digital").
- **Direction 2 (Selected)**: Physical "Mood Board" Scrapbook.
- **Why**: Overlapping images, varied rotations, and subtle drop shadows make it feel like a physical desk in a studio where ideas are pinned and analyzed. It feels like "The Craft" in action.
